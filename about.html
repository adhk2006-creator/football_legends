The topic

About Football Legends

Football Legends is a small showcase site about the history of football and the players who came to define it. Below is a quick look at where the game came from, why certain players earn the word “legend,” and how this particular site was put together.

Origins

The beautiful game

Football in something like its modern form was codified in England in 1863, when a set of shared rules turned dozens of local kicking games into one sport. From there it travelled with sailors, workers and missionaries to almost every country on earth, taking root fastest in places where a ball and a patch of open ground were the only equipment required.

That low barrier to entry is a big part of why football became the world's most played and watched sport — it can be played on a beach at sunset just as easily as it can be played in a 90,000-seat stadium.

Why legends matter

Every generation of football produces a handful of players whose skill, longevity or sheer flair change how the game is talked about. This site focuses on four of them: Pelé, the first global superstar of the sport; Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi, whose rivalry defined two decades of the modern game; and Neymar, one of the most recognisable players to come out of Brazil's street-football tradition.

  • Pelé — three World Cup wins with Brazil (1958, 1962, 1970)
  • Lionel Messi — record-extending Ballon d'Or wins and a 2022 World Cup title
  • Cristiano Ronaldo — one of football's all-time leading goalscorers
  • Neymar Jr. — a symbol of Brazilian flair on and off the pitch
The project

About this site

Football Legends is a static, hand-coded HTML and CSS website built as a web development learning project. Every page shares one external stylesheet for consistent navigation and typography, plus a page-specific stylesheet for its own layout — no JavaScript or server-side code is used anywhere on the site.

The photo grid on the Gallery page doubles as a small demonstration of image editing: each of the four players appears as an untouched original alongside grayscale, sepia and high-contrast versions of the same photo.
